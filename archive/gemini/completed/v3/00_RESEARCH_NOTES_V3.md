## Research-to-design conclusions

1. V2's "EXACTLY 2 SENTENCES" is replaced by a variable sentence budget because conversation turns are recipient- and sequence-sensitive rather than mechanically uniform (R06-R10).
2. A hook is defined as a continuation function, not a compulsory question. This follows from adjacency-pair logic and responsiveness research: a useful next move can be an acknowledgment, observation, self-share, callback or low-pressure continuation (R01-R10).
3. Question frequency is constrained because questions create response obligations; question-heavy output can turn conversation into an interview. The engine therefore tracks recent questions and can choose a statement or WAIT (R02, R07).
4. Reciprocity is tracked behaviorally, not as an attraction score. The ledger records observable interactional contributions and only uses them for pressure calibration (R01-R04, R06).
5. "AI smell" is turned into testable failure conditions: generic scaffolding, unsupported inference, repeated template openings, random topic jumps, unnatural symmetry, excess meta-empathy, and mismatch to the latest turn (R01-R10, R15).
6. Vietnam-specific evidence is used only for contextual calibration, not as proof of individual behavior. Tinder's study directly includes Vietnam, while Tuổi Trẻ reports a Vietnam survey; both have sample/method limitations that remain explicit (R12-R13).

## What is not claimed

- No source establishes the user's proposed 2:1 statement-to-question ratio, hook taxonomy, flirt ladder, or exact reply-length thresholds as scientifically validated constants. These are ENGINE DESIGN POLICIES requested by the user and are marked as product rules, not empirical laws.
- No source justifies inferring attraction, attachment style, romantic intent, or dislike from a single message.
- No U.S.-only dating statistic is treated as a Vietnam prevalence estimate.
- Company-commissioned dating reports are treated as contextual/market evidence, not independent causal evidence.
